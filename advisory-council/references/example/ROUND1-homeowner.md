# ROUND 1: Mainstream buyer, home owner (homeowner)

**Dossier:** DOSSIER-01-alert-subscription.md. **Addenda read:** none.
**Round 1, independent:** no other member's report was read before this one was written.

**Position:** Oppose A as drafted; keep phone push alerts free for every buyer, raise the price openly (B), and sell text or call escalation and extra contacts as an optional tier at no more than 2 a month.
**Confidence:** medium

## 1. Position and why

- I paid 49 for the alert to my phone. Under A a non-payer keeps a siren and in-app status on home Wi-Fi only (fact, dossier F6). A siren in an empty house protects nothing. That breaks my red line whether or not it is disclosed.
- 3.50 a month is 42 a year; over three years a 49 sensor costs 175, against 55 once under B (estimate: 3.50 x 36 + 49). It shows on my card statement every month, which is exactly when I cancel things.
- The cost problem is mostly text messages: 268,000 of 412,000 (65%) of 2025 alert cost (fact, F5). Without texts, alerts cost about 0.61 per connected device a year (estimate below). B's extra 6 covers that for years; charging for the expensive channel puts its cost on the buyers who choose it.
- Proposed option E (not on the dossier's list): B, plus an optional paid tier for text or call escalation and extra contacts, which the insurer (C, F12) could pay for its policyholders. This matches the condition on which my seat would accept a paid tier.
- I want the full price before I buy, not a free trial that turns into a charge (F7's pilot used a 30-day trial).

## 2. Where the dossier is wrong, incomplete or mis-posed

- Mis-posed: "remote alerts" is treated as one thing, but most of the cost is one channel (F5). The question should be which channel, if any, is paid for.
- Price table: the platform's 29.99 is labelled "per month" but sourced to the platform's "annual plan". If it is yearly, that bundle is about 2.50 a month, less than A's 3.50. This must be checked before anyone calls A cheap next to the platform.
- F5's 1.75 divides a whole year's cost by the December base (F4), the year's largest point, so it understates cost per device. E5 then applies the text supplier's 20% rise to all of 1.75 instead of the 65% it covers: 0.35 x 1.75 + 0.65 x 1.75 x 1.2 = about 1.98, not 2.10, so E5 is about 690,000 on its own base. The two errors partly offset.
- E1's 1.4 million is year-end recurring revenue on a full year of 115,000 units, though A starts in spring 2027; it is revenue, while E3 for B is margin, so the two are not on one baseline. Its arithmetic checks (115,000 x 0.31 x 0.94 x 42 = about 1.41 million).
- F7's 31% is one UK online figure from a summary slide covering four cohorts; the slide does not say whether it is pooled, or whether November trials had ended. F8's 1.1% mixes monthly and annual billing, so it likely understates churn on a monthly plan.
- F10 and F11: 14 weeks of partner stock against 90 days' notice (about 13 weeks) means boxes saying "No subscription." can still be on shelves when A starts. The dossier does not say what boxes printed after October 2025 say.
- F2, F3 and E4 arithmetic checks (recomputed by hand); E4 counts one year's sales, not policyholders with an active sensor.

## 4. Risks from this seat

- A: the quiet failure is an expired card; alerts stop and nobody notices until the floor is wet. The loud one is discovering the fee at setup, then returns and bad reviews.
- B: small; 6 more is visible before purchase. Some buyers may drift to 18-25 imports with weaker alerting.
- D: the company struggles and cuts alerts later with no warning, worse for me than an honest price now.
- E: if push is the channel that fails (F9 does not say), a free push tier gives false comfort.

## 5. The cheapest decisive step

Split the Q3 missed-alert tickets (F9, already held) by channel: push, text, or device offline. A day's work, no new data. Decided now: if more than half are push failures that text would have caught, free push is not a reliable safety alert, and I would accept a paid tier that includes text fallback, if it is on the box and costs no more than 2 a month. Otherwise option E stands.

## 6. Conditions and red lines

Support needs: a phone alert for a leak without paying; the full price on the box, listing and app store page before purchase; warnings and a grace period before any paid feature stops on a failed payment. I will not accept A as drafted, or "No subscription." stock sold under new terms. Whether a sold device's features may change, and the rules on sign-up and cancellation in four countries, are questions for qualified counsel.

## 7. One line

Keep phone alerts free, put the price rise on the box, and charge only for extras such as text and call escalation, at no more than 2 a month.
