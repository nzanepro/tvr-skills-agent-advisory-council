# ROUND 1: Competitor perspective, large smart-home platform archetype (platform)

**Dossier:** DOSSIER-01-alert-subscription.md. **Addenda read:** none.
**Round 1, independent:** no other member's report was read before this one was written.

**Position:** Do not launch option A; a platform would use it to sell against Fennick, so raise the price (B), keep push alerts free, offer text-message fallback as an optional paid add-on, and pursue the insurer (C) alongside.
**Confidence:** medium

This seat is a competitor-perspective archetype built from public-type reasoning about bundle pricing, not any real company, and it states no real company's plans or figures.

## 1. Position and why

1. Fennick's clearest difference from a platform bundle is "no hub, no subscription" (F10 packaging says so). Option A gives that up. A large platform in this position would likely answer with "leak alerts included in your plan", and it would not need to change its price.
2. Under A, Fennick sits between two rivals: dearer than a free-alert import at 18-25 (section 2 table), yet charging a subscription like the platform while covering one device instead of the whole home. A platform would likely aim at buyers with two or more sensors, where per-device pricing looks worst (section 3 table).
3. A platform gains little against option B. At 55 with free alerts, Fennick stays the simple option for someone who wants one device. That is a low-end nuisance to a platform, not an opening.
4. The cost problem is mostly text messages, not alerts in general. Text-message fallback is 65% of alert cost (F5). If only that channel becomes a paid add-on (an option not on the list), Fennick targets the cost driver and keeps the claim a platform would find hardest to attack: a leak alert reaches your phone for free.
5. A large platform would likely court the same insurers. The letter of intent (F12) is non-binding, so waiting risks losing it (assumption).

## 2. Where the dossier is wrong, incomplete or mis-posed

- **The price comparison is not on one basis.** The platform row says 29.99 "per month" but cites the platform's annual plan. It is also a price per home covering every device, while Fennick's 3.50 is per device. The table cannot show what a buyer comparing the offers would actually pay, and whether 29.99 is a month or a year reverses the result (table below).
- **Take-up is one figure without a method.** F7's 31% comes from one slide. It does not say whether this is the pooled rate for all four cohorts or one cohort's rate. It counts conversion after a free trial, from UK online buyers only, yet E1 applies it to retail buyers and all four countries.
- **The churn figure is mislabelled for its use.** F8 is warranty-plan churn blended across monthly and annual billing. E1 uses it for a plan billed monthly.
- **Baselines are mixed.** E1 uses the 2027 plan of 115,000 units, while E3 and E4 use 2025 actuals (112,000 and 83,200). F5 divides a full year's cost by the December base, which understates the average cost per device. E5 applies the 20% rise to the whole of 2.10, but only the text-message 65% rises: 1.75 x (1 + 0.65 x 0.20) = 1.98. That gives about 690,000 against the 740,000 in E5.
- **E1 is a run rate.** The 1.4 million is annual recurring revenue at the end of 2027, not 2027 revenue, and it is before the 3% payment fees and 60,000 running cost (E2). The arithmetic itself checks out: F2 and F3 each sum to 112,000; E1 comes to 1.41 million, E3 to 578,000 and E4 to 159,744.

## 4. Risks from this seat

- **A:** a platform takes the multi-sensor buyers and the landlords, and imports take the price-led buyers. This failure is quiet: it shows up as a slow fall in units rather than complaints, and E6 assumes it away.
- **B:** a platform barely reacts, but imports widen the price gap from 24-31 to 30-37.
- **C alone:** Fennick could lose the insurer to a bigger bundle before the contract is signed. **D:** alert cost rises with every sale (E5).

## 5. The cheapest decisive step

The first step costs almost nothing: marketing re-reads the published price pages to say whether 29.99 is monthly or annual, and whether it is per home. Then add a one-question in-app survey to the connected base (F4): "Do you use a smart-home hub or platform plan?" If fewer than 25% of respondents say yes and 29.99 turns out to be a monthly price, a platform bundle is a poor substitute for most Fennick buyers. In that case I would drop my competitive objection to A.

## 6. Conditions and red lines

- **Conditions for supporting A:** the survey result above, plus a checkout test showing that completion falls by no more than 5% when the plan is shown.
- **What I will not accept:** any option that removes free push alerts, because that hands a platform its marketing line. Packaging claims and subscription rules are questions for qualified counsel, not the council.

## 7. One line

Keep free push alerts as the thing a platform cannot undercut, and cover cost with a price rise, a paid text-message add-on and the insurer, not a paywall.
