# ROUND 1: Chief Financial Officer (cfo)

**Dossier:** DOSSIER-01-alert-subscription.md. **Addenda read:** none.
**Round 1, independent:** no other member's report was read before this one was written.

**Position:** A paid tier for new customers, yes in principle, but not option A as drafted and not on the pilot slide: keep push alerts free, put the text-message cost in the paid tier, and release the 180,000 build only after the pooled pilot data and a monthly-billed churn figure are in.
**Confidence:** medium

## 1. Position and why

- D fails my red line: alert cost grows with every device sold, and E5 has it near 740,000 in 2027 against a 2025 operating profit of 140,000 (F1).
- B is cash now with no build (about 0.58 million a year, E3) but does not cap the cost. At about 2 a device a year and 15% of the base going offline yearly (E5), a device costs roughly 13 in alerts over its life; B adds 4 to 6 once. B is a bridge, not a fix.
- A pays back the build fast if unit sales hold (my model: 7 to 10 months even at half the pilot take-up). But it beats B on cash to end 2028 only if pooled take-up is above about 22% to 25%. That is uncomfortably close to a single slide's 31%.
- Proposed option E (not on the dossier's list): push alerts stay free; text and call escalation, which is 65% of alert cost (F5), becomes the paid tier. It caps the fastest-growing cost on every new device, even for non-payers, and stays out of the "safety feature behind a paywall" argument.
- C is worth a meeting and no build money until the insurer shares real terms (F12 is non-binding).

## 2. Where the dossier is wrong, incomplete or mis-posed

- **E1 overstates end-2027 recurring revenue.** It counts a full year of sales, but the launch is spring 2027 and there is a 30-day trial. At the same 31% and 1.1%, my model gives about 0.96 million at end 2027, not 1.4 million. That is a year-end run rate, not 2027 cash.
- **F7 may be one point, not the series.** Four cohorts give one figure. The slide does not say it is pooled, and the November cohort's trial ended in December, the month of the slide, so its conversion may be partly unobserved. The full report was not searched (section 6).
- **F8 is the wrong churn for A.** It blends monthly- and annual-billed plans on a warranty product. Annual payers cannot churn mid-term, so the monthly-billed rate is probably higher.
- **E5's baselines are mixed.** The 20% rise is applied to all alert cost, not just text (1.98 a device, not 2.10). F5's 1.75 divides a full year's cost by the December base; on an average 2025 base it is about 2.16. Net result: 2027 cost could be anywhere from about 690,000 to 850,000.
- **E3 uses 2025 units (112,000), E1 the 2027 plan (115,000).** E4 treats one year's UK and Ireland sales as a stock of policyholders, but the insurer pays per active sensor, so its base is the installed base.
- **Missing:** gross margin per unit, without which the cost of lost sales under E6 cannot be valued. The price table's platform row puts an "annual plan" source under a per-month column, so the 29.99 basis needs confirming.
- **Mis-posed goal:** "alert cost covered by margin by end 2028" does not say whether this is the whole cost or the increase over 2025.

## 4. Risks from this seat

The quiet failure under A is lost unit sales at checkout and in retail (E6 is unmeasured, and retail packaging promises "no subscription", F10). A 5% volume drop would not show up until 2027 unit numbers came in, after the build is spent. Under B the quiet failure is cost growth hidden inside a healthier-looking gross margin. Under C it is management time on a non-binding letter.

## 5. The cheapest decisive step

Before the board meeting on 14 April, pull the full pilot report or raw export: take-up per cohort and pooled, measured only after each trial ended, and checkout completion against unexposed buyers. Split the F8 billing export by billing frequency. This is desk work on data already held. **Result decided now:** if pooled take-up is below 22%, or completion fell by more than 5%, I move to B plus a paid text tier with no 180,000 billing build. If pooled take-up is 25% or more, completion is flat and monthly churn is at most 3%, I back the build, with the paid tier shaped as option E.

## 6. Conditions and red lines

I support a build only if payback is 18 months or less on the pooled pilot data, with alert cost capped on every new device. I will not accept payback beyond 24 months, or an option that leaves per-device alert cost uncapped as the base grows (D, and B on its own).

## 7. One line

Cap the text-message cost now, bridge with the price rise, and spend 180,000 on billing only once the whole pilot, not its headline slide, shows take-up above about 25%.
