# DOSSIER 01: Subscription for remote alerts

**Convened:** 2026-03-02. **Question:** Should Fennick Home charge new customers for remote phone
alerts from spring 2027, take another route, or keep alerts free?

This is the identical factual record every member works from. **Check it and report where it is
wrong, incomplete or mis-posed.** Later numbered addenda (ADDENDUM-01A, 01B, ...) win where
they conflict with this file. The company, its partners and every number are fictional. Money is
in local currency units (pounds in the UK, euros elsewhere); ledger totals are converted to pounds
at the 2025 budget rate. The prices 49 and 3.50 apply in every country.

## 0. The question in one line

Should remote alerts from the Fennick leak sensor move to a paid subscription for new customers
from spring 2027, and if not, what else (if anything) should change?

## 1. Options on the table

| Option | What it means | Reversible? | Rough cost / time |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Subscription, new customers | New buyers from spring 2027 pay 3.50 a month for remote alerts; existing buyers keep them free | Hard to reverse once billed customers exist | Build 180,000, six months (E2) |
| B. One-off price rise | Device price rises from 49 to 55; alerts stay free for everyone | Yes; the price can be cut again | Packaging and listings only |
| C. Insurer-paid bundle | The insurance partner pays for alerts for its policyholders; others as today or as A | Depends on the contract term | Partner integration; contract not yet negotiated |
| D. Do nothing | Alerts stay free; the company absorbs the rising alert cost | Yes | Alert cost grows with the installed base (E5) |

The council may reshape, combine or add options.

## 2. Market or domain

- **Buyers:** owner-occupiers buying one or two sensors, and small landlords buying several.
  Channels: online direct and two retail partners (one DIY chain, one electricals chain).
- **Geography:** the UK plus Ireland, the Netherlands and Belgium (F3).
- **Competitors, by type (archetypes, no named company):** large smart-home platforms that include
  leak alerts in a home-monitoring plan; low-cost imported sensors with a basic app and no
  subscription. No market-size figure is used: the only one found was a vendor press release with
  no method.

## 3. Goal, constraints and success measures

- **Goal:** cover the rising cost of running alerts without losing buyers' trust.
- **Measure of success:** alert cost covered by recurring or one-off margin by end of 2028; 2027
  unit sales no more than 5% below the plan of 115,000; tickets and returns no higher than 2025.
- **Constraints:** 40 staff; a six-month billing build (E2); 90 days' notice to retail partners
  (F11); existing customers' alerts stay free; legal questions go to qualified counsel.
- **Who decides, and by when:** the CEO and the board, at the board meeting on 14 April 2026. A
  spring 2027 launch of option A would need the build to start by September 2026.

## 6. What is not known

- **Price sensitivity (option B).** No price test since launch in 2023. Searched the sales ledger
  (one list price throughout) and the marketing test log (no price tests recorded).
- **Whether showing the plan at checkout lowered purchases.** Not on the pilot summary slide; only
  the slide was searched, not the full pilot report.
- **How many sold sensors stay online.** The heartbeat log has not been analysed by month of sale.
- **Landlord share of buyers.** Only multi-unit online orders are visible (F13); retail buyers
  cannot be identified.
- **Share of buyers who already own a smart-home platform.** Not collected at checkout or in the app.
- **The legal position** on selling a device whose remote alerts need a subscription, on changing
  features after sale, and on subscription sign-up and cancellation in the four countries. Not
  researched; no counsel has been consulted.
- **The insurer's real terms and any claims effect.** The letter is non-binding; no claims data
  has been shared.

## 7. Positions already held

The CEO favours option A. From the January 2026 planning notes: "Alerts are the product. If people
value them they will pay 3.50 a month for them; if they won't, we have been giving away the wrong
thing." This is a position for the council to test, not a premise.

## 8. What the council is asked

1. Should remote alerts become a paid subscription for new customers, and if so in what form
   (A-D, a combination, or something else)?
2. What take-up and churn would a subscription need to beat options B and D, and does the evidence
   show it?
3. What does a buyer who does not pay get, and is that acceptable for a safety device?
4. Is the insurer-paid bundle worth pursuing, alone or alongside another option?
5. Which legal and retail-channel questions must be settled before any change, and by whom?
6. What is the cheapest step that would settle the biggest open question, and what result would
   change the decision?
