# ROUND 1: Small landlord with 5-20 units (landlord)

**Dossier:** DOSSIER-01-alert-subscription.md. **Addenda read:** none.
**Round 1, independent:** no other member's report was read before this one was written.

**Position:** Against option A as a per-device fee; back option B now, plus a new option E (a portfolio plan at no more than 1 a unit a month with agent and second-contact alerts), and pursue C only if it covers landlord policies.
**Confidence:** medium

## 1. Position and why

- A per-device fee scales straight through my red line: 12 units x 3.50 x 12 is 504 a year (dossier landlord card), every year, for a feature I bought the device for.
- The fee is about 20 times what an alert costs to run: 42 a year against about 2.10 per device a year (E5, F5). A portfolio price of 1 a unit a month (12 a year) would still be several times the cost.
- Option B charges me 6 once per unit, which covers roughly three years of alert cost at 2.10 (estimate). I can budget a one-off; I cancel or replace recurring per-device charges.
- Without paying, F6 leaves me a siren and in-app status on "the home Wi-Fi", which is my tenant's network, not mine. In an empty flat nobody hears the siren, so for my use the unpaid device is close to useless.
- What I would pay for is not in any option: one bill per portfolio, a view across properties, and alerts to my letting agent or a second contact.

## 2. Where the dossier is wrong, incomplete or mis-posed

- **E5 applies the 20% rise to the whole cost.** The rise is on the text-message supplier, 65% of cost (F5). Recomputed: 1.75 x (0.35 + 0.65 x 1.2) = 1.98, not 2.10, so 2027 cost is about 692,000, not 740,000.
- **F5's 1.75 per device divides a full year's cost by the December base (F4),** a single end-of-year point. The average 2025 base was smaller, so the true per-device cost was higher; the dossier should state the average base.
- **Mixed baselines:** E1 and E5 use the 2027 plan (115,000 units); E3 uses 2025 actuals (112,000); E4 uses 2025 country units. Options are compared on different volume years.
- **F13 is not a landlord share.** 11% of online units is 7,150 units, 6.4% of all units (my arithmetic). Some five-plus orders are not landlords, and landlords buying fewer units or in retail are missed. The dossier says so in section 6 but no option is sized for this segment.
- **Pricing unit is undefined.** Option A never says whether 3.50 is per device or per account; E1 multiplies by units, so it assumes per device.
- **F7 is one slide, UK online only.** It may not be pooled across all four cohorts, and it cannot show multi-unit buyers' take-up. F8 churn is from a warranty plan billed monthly or annually, not a monthly alert plan.
- **Price table basis:** the platform's 29.99 is labelled per month but sourced to an "annual plan"; it may be an annual figure in a monthly column.
- **Missing option:** a portfolio or multi-property plan (option E above). Also missing: whether landlord insurance policies fall under the insurer's letter (F12).

## 4. Risks from this seat

- A: quiet churn. I will not complain; I will buy the next 10 sensors elsewhere, and no ticket or return shows it.
- B: small and visible; 6 a unit is tolerable. C: may exclude landlord policies entirely. D: the company stops funding alerts later with less notice.
- Any option: whether a landlord may receive alerts from a tenant's home, and on what consent, is a question for qualified counsel; I cannot answer it.

## 5. The cheapest decisive step

Split the existing pilot data (F7) and the online order export (F13) by order size, and count app accounts with devices on more than one home network. Desk work, no new test. If pilot buyers of five or more units took the plan at or above the overall rate at 3.50 per device, I drop my opposition to A (keeping the second-contact condition). If their take-up is below half the overall rate, per-device A loses this segment and option E should be priced before any launch.

## 6. Conditions and red lines

- Support A only with a portfolio price of no more than about 1 a unit a month, or an insurer discount that covers the fee.
- Red lines: per-device pricing that scales linearly beyond about five units; no way to send alerts to an agent or second contact.

## 7. One line

Do not charge landlords 3.50 per sensor per month for a service costing about 2 a year; raise the price once, and offer a portfolio plan with agent alerts.
