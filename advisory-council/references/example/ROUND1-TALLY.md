## C. Where the council agrees

- **On the action:** no seat backs option A as drafted; all six would raise the device price in
  some form; five (all but insurer) would keep push alerts free and sell something extra; four
  (cfo, homeowner, platform, advocate) name text or call escalation as that extra. None backs D.
- **Not on the reasons.** The cfo seat objects to A's evidence and payback, not to charging; the
  homeowner and advocate seats object to a safety function behind a paywall; the platform seat
  to losing the "no subscription" difference; the landlord seat to per-device scaling; the insurer
  seat to its payment becoming the switch that keeps alerts on.
- **Unanimity check.** Six against A is a warning sign. The contrarian seat (advocate) did argue
  its case. But the dossier recorded the CEO's leaning for A and no seat was built to argue for a
  recurring revenue line as strategy; only the cfo seat came near it. Option A is therefore
  carried below at full strength, and the gap is noted in section E.

## D. Where it splits

1. **Should remote alerts ever be a paid subscription?** The cfo seat: yes in principle, if the
   pooled data pass its tests (take-up at least 25%, completion flat, monthly churn at most 3%).
   The homeowner and advocate seats: never for the only remote alert; that is a red line. The
   action for spring 2027 does not depend on this, since both sides accept free push plus a paid
   extra now; whether a full subscription stays on the table later does. Round 2 question 1.
2. **Should text fallback leave the free tier?** Cfo, platform, advocate and homeowner seats put
   it in the paid tier (it is 65% of alert cost, F5). The insurer seat wants alerts that work in
   every insured home, and the homeowner seat's own test says text belongs in the free tier if most
   missed alerts are push failures. No channel split of the missed-alert tickets exists (V33). The
   action depends on this. Round 2 question 2.
3. **2027 cost of doing nothing:** about 690,000 (landlord) against up to 850,000 (insurer, cfo);
   recorded in ADDENDUM-01A. The action does not depend on it: at either figure D fails the cfo
   seat's red line and the cost rise (278,000-438,000) is below E3's 578,000 if volume holds.
4. **How the insurer pays:** per unit sold (dossier F12, E4) or per sensor online and alerting,
   with a one-off activation payment first (insurer seat). Affects option C's size, not the choice
   between the other options.
5. **Multi-unit pricing:** per device (A as drafted) or per portfolio at about 1 a unit a month
   (landlord). Round 2 question 4.

## F. Options for Round 2

Always include the strongest minority position as an option. "Other" is always allowed.

- **A.** Option A as proposed: 3.50 a month for remote alerts for new customers from spring 2027.
  Backed by: no seat in Round 1; carried as the CEO's position to test. Cost: build 180,000,
  running 60,000 a year. Main risk: on items 1, 2 and 7 the run rate is 0.41-0.53 million, and
  completion was 3.7 points lower in the pilot (item 9).
- **B.** Price rise to 55 for everyone; all alerts, text included, stay free; no paid tier and no
  billing build; insurer pays on results for its policyholders. Backed by: insurer (closest).
  Cost: packaging, listings, 90 days' notice. Main risk: volume untested; the per-device alert
  cost stays uncapped as the base grows.
- **C.** Price rise to 55; push alerts free for every buyer; an optional paid escalation tier (text
  or call escalation, extra contacts, a portfolio view with a per-portfolio price for multi-unit
  buyers) at no more than 2 a month, shown before purchase in every channel; the insurer may pay
  the tier for its policyholders on results. Backed by: homeowner, advocate, platform, landlord.
  Cost: a smaller billing build (not yet estimated) plus packaging. Main risk: tier take-up is
  unknown; free push gives false comfort if push is what fails.
- **D.** Staged and gated (strongest minority): the price rise and a paid escalation tier now as
  in C, with the 180,000 build released in stages, and a full subscription for remote alerts kept
  open for 2028 if a new pilot shows pooled take-up of at least 25%, flat completion and monthly
  churn of at most 3%. Backed by: cfo. Cost: as C, plus a second pilot. Main risk: keeps the
  paywall question alive, which the homeowner and advocate seats call a red line.
