# ROUND 1: Consumer advocate (argues against the change) (advocate)

**Dossier:** DOSSIER-01-alert-subscription.md. **Addenda read:** none.
**Round 1, independent:** no other member's report was read before this one was written.

**Position:** Against option A as drafted: keep a remote push alert free for every buyer, raise the device price openly (B) and sell text or call escalation as an optional tier shown before purchase (option E, new).
**Confidence:** medium

## 1. Position and why

1. Option A puts the safety job behind a paywall. Under F6 a non-payer keeps a siren and Wi-Fi-only status, so a leak while nobody is home reaches nobody, which is the case the device is bought for (assumption, from the homeowner card). That is my red line.
2. The failure is quiet: after the 30-day trial (F7) remote alerts stop and the buyer may not notice until the flood. A price rise fails loudly, at the till, where the buyer can still say no.
3. The cost sits mostly in text fallback, 268,000 of 412,000 (F5). **Option E (not on the dossier's list):** push alerts free for all buyers; text or call escalation and extra contacts as an optional paid tier, priced on the box and listing; combined with B. It targets the expensive channel without removing the safety function.
4. B is the open route my card prefers: full price known before buying, reversible (dossier section 1), and E3's 0.58 million exceeds the rise in alert cost under D (740,000 minus 412,000 = 328,000, my arithmetic on E5 and F5), if volume holds, which is untested.
5. Counting my own costs: B's volume risk is unmeasured (section 6), existing buyers stay a growing cost, and a company that cannot fund alerts protects nobody. I am not arguing for D.

## 2. Where the dossier is wrong, incomplete or mis-posed

- **E1 is a year-end run-rate on a full year of sales.** A spring launch gives about nine months of sign-ups; my recompute gives an end-2027 run-rate of about 0.95-1.1 million and cash billed in 2027 of about 0.36 million, before 3% fees, 60,000 running cost and the 180,000 build (E2).
- **31% is one slide figure** (F7): UK online only, after a free trial, not stated as pooled across the four cohorts; the full report was not searched (section 6). Retail (42% of units, F2) and three countries are untested. **F8** is a warranty plan billed monthly or annually, blended; it is not a monthly alert plan.
- **The platform row looks mislabelled:** 29.99 sits in the per-month column but is sourced to an "annual plan". If it is 29.99 a year (2.50 a month), A at 42 a year costs more than a whole-home bundle and the table flatters A.
- **E5 applies the 20% text rise to the whole cost:** only 65% is text, so 1.75 x (0.35 + 0.65 x 1.2) = 1.98, about 690,000 not 740,000. Against that, F5's 1.75 divides a year's cost by the December base (one point, not the year's average), which understates per-device cost. D in 2027 could be 0.69-0.85 million; the dossier shows one figure.
- **Mixed baselines:** E1 uses 115,000 planned units, E3 and E4 use 2025 units (112,000; 83,200); E1 is revenue, E3 is margin.
- **Missing:** what current packaging and listings say (F10 covers only boxes printed before October 2025); what pilot non-payers lost and were told, when as 2025 buyers their alerts are meant to stay free; a 2025 ticket baseline for the success measure, when only Q3 is in the record (F9).

## 4. Risks from this seat

- **A:** alerts end silently after the trial; if current artwork still says "No subscription" (F10), about 14 weeks of stock against 90 days' notice (F11) means a buyer can pay 49 for a box that promises what the device will not do. A paid service with about 832 missed-alert tickets a quarter invites refund claims.
- **B:** a loud but fair cost; the risk is volume. **C:** alert data shared without clear consent; alerts that end when a policy lapses. **D:** the quiet failure is the company: alerts cut later to save money.
- For qualified counsel, not this seat: selling a safety device whose remote alert needs a subscription; free-trial sign-up and cancellation in four countries; the "No subscription" claim on stock still in the channel.

## 5. The cheapest decisive step

Re-cut the 2025 supplier invoices by channel and by month against the heartbeat base, to cost push-only alerting per device (analyst days, no build). Decided now: under about 1.00 per device a year at 2027 prices, E plus B is fundable and I stay against A. Over about 1.50, the cost is not in text fallback, E fails its funding test, and I would accept a larger open price rise or a disclosed optional tier, still never alerts removed for non-payers.

## 6. Conditions and red lines

- I support a paid tier only if a non-payer keeps at least one remote leak alert channel and the tier is optional and shown before purchase online, on the box and at the shelf.
- Red lines: remote leak alerts behind a paywall; any change reaching existing buyers or unsold "free alerts" stock; alerts stopping on a failed card payment without warning and a grace period.

## 7. One line

Charge openly for the box and for the expensive extras, but never sell a leak sensor that stays silent when nobody is home because the buyer did not subscribe.
