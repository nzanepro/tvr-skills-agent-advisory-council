# ROUND 1: Home-insurance partner (insurer)

**Dossier:** DOSSIER-01-alert-subscription.md. **Addenda read:** none.
**Round 1, independent:** no other member's report was read before this one was written.

**Position:** Keep remote alerts working for every buyer (B, not A); pursue C only as a pay-on-results pilot, per sensor online and alerting, not per unit sold.
**Confidence:** medium

## 1. Position and why

- My book gains only from sensors that alert when nobody is home. Under A a non-payer keeps a siren and Wi-Fi status only (dossier F6); at the pilot's 31% take-up (F7), about two in three new sensors would give no away-from-home alert, which is when escape-of-water damage grows largest (assumption, partner claims experience).
- A makes my payment the switch that turns safety on. A failed card, a failed policy match or a lapsed consent quietly turns alerts off in an insured home. I would rather fund something that still works without me.
- The 2.00 in F12 is a ceiling for discussion, not an offer. On my own figures (section 3) a 20% claims cut is worth about 0.80 a policy a month. I will not pay 2.00 a month per unit sold on hope.
- This conflicts with Fennick's revenue: under B my money buys less than under A, so C yields less than E4. My offer instead (new option, E): a one-off activation payment per subsidised sensor still online at six months, a consented data share for a matched comparison, and a monthly fee only once claims fall.

## 2. Where the dossier is wrong, incomplete or mis-posed

- E4's arithmetic is right (83,200 x 8% x 2.00 x 12 = 159,744) but the base is wrong: F12 pays per policyholder with an active sensor, E4 counts units sold. Multi-unit buyers (F13) and sensors gone offline both inflate it, and the 8% is the partner's market share assumed to equal its share of Fennick buyers.
- F5's 1.75 divides a full-year cost by the December connected base, one point, not the year's average. On an average base the 2025 cost is nearer 2.2 a device (estimate, section 3).
- E5 applies the notified 20% rise to the whole alert cost; it applies to text fallback, 65% of cost (F5). Blended rise is about 13%: 1.98, not 2.10, on the dossier's own 1.75. The two errors pull opposite ways.
- E5's 15% a year going offline has no source; section 6 says the heartbeat log has not been analysed. It is the number my seat needs most.
- Nothing measures alert delivery. F9 counts complaints (21% of 3,960, about 832 in Q3), not failures. I searched the dossier for a delivery success rate and found none; that does not mean Fennick has none.
- E1's 31% is one UK online pilot applied to every channel and country, and the slide does not say it is pooled across the four cohorts; F8's churn mixes monthly and annual billing.
- C is posed as a revenue line for Fennick; for me it is a claims experiment and needs a measurement plan.

## 4. Risks from this seat

- A: the quiet failure. Most new homes lose away alerts; insured homes lose them when payment or matching fails, and nobody notices until a claim arrives.
- C: I pay for boxes left in drawers; set-up drop-off and consent friction shrink the active base. Consent for sharing alert data and the rules on distributing a bundled insurance benefit in the UK and Ireland are questions for qualified counsel.
- D: Fennick cannot fund alerts, trims text fallback (65% of spend, F5) and delivery quietly worsens.
- B: least risk to my book; the risk is volume loss with no price test behind it (E3, section 6).

## 5. The cheapest decisive step

Analyse the existing heartbeat log (F4) by month of sale or activation, with one month of the supplier's delivery receipts: share of sensors online six months after sale, and share of alerts delivered within five minutes. Decided now: if at least 70% are online at six months and at least 98% of alerts arrive in time, I will fund a 12-month matched-comparison pilot on option E terms; if under 60% are online, I withdraw any monthly fee and offer one-off activation payments only.

## 6. Conditions and red lines

- Support needs: payment per sensor online and alerting, audited from the heartbeat log; explicit customer consent; a 12-month matched comparison showing at least 20% fewer escape-of-water claims before any monthly fee near 2.00.
- Red lines: paying for sensors installed but not alerting; any use of alert data without clear consent; a design in which my non-payment switches off a policyholder's alerts.

## 7. One line

Keep alerts working in every home and let me pay for measured results, not for units sold or for a paywall I would then have to hold open.
