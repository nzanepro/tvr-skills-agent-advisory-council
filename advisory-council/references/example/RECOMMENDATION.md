# RECOMMENDATION: Subscription for remote alerts

**Recommendation:** Do not adopt option A, the CEO's leaning; instead raise the device price to 55, keep push alerts free for every buyer, and add an optional escalation tier in stages, moving text fallback into it only if a channel check shows free push alerts are reliable.
**Council vote:** option C: 5 of 6 seats, weighted 6 of 7. Option B: 1 of 6, weighted 1 of 7. No seat voted A or D. **Dissent:** insurer (B).
**Confidence:** medium, because the corrected pilot and churn figures point one way, but price sensitivity, push reliability and every legal question are unmeasured.
**Decision owner:** the user. The council is a simulation that advises; it does not decide.

## 1. What to do

Option C, staged. The CEO's preferred option A drew no vote in either round: its corrected
end-2027 run rate is 0.41-0.53 million, not 1.4 million (ADDENDUM-01A item 7).

1. Before the board on 14 April: run the three desk checks in section 6 (channel split of missed
   alerts, heartbeat log by month of sale, what 2025 pilot non-payers lost).
2. Price rise to 55 for new units, after a randomised online price test; 90 days' notice to both
   retail partners (F11, item 10). Push alerts stay free; text stays free until the channel check.
3. Get a fixed quote for the tier (text or call escalation, extra contacts, a portfolio view with a
   price of no more than 1 a unit a month for five or more units) at no more than 2 a month, billed
   yearly on the warranty plan's billing. Build only if it pays back within 18 months.
4. Negotiate the insurer's part as pay on results, per sensor online and alerting (item 8).

|---|---|---:|---:|
| C | cfo, homeowner, landlord, platform, advocate | 5 of 6 | 6 of 7 |
| B | insurer | 1 of 6 | 1 of 7 |

## 2. Why

1. **Option A fails on the corrected record.** Take-up was 18.6% pooled and 14.4% without a launch
   offer, falling by cohort; monthly-billed churn is 3.8% (items 1, 2, 7). Completion fell from
   73.9% to 70.2% and returns rose from 2.6% to 3.9% in the pilot (item 9, not randomised).
   The cfo seat's own pre-declared test moved it off the subscription path (ROUND2-cfo section 1).
2. **A non-payer under A owns a silent sensor** (F6). Free push keeps the safety job for every
   buyer and keeps "free alerts to your phone" true, which a platform bundle at 29.99 a year cannot
   undercut on price (item 3; ROUND2-homeowner section 1, ROUND2-platform section 1).
3. **Doing nothing fails the cost test.** 2027 alert cost is about 690,000-850,000 against a 2025
   operating profit of 140,000 (items 5, 6, recorded disagreement). Text is 65% of it (F5), so the
   tier targets the cost driver (ROUND2-cfo section 1, ROUND2-landlord section 1).

## 4. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Who raised it | Likelihood / impact | Mitigation or early warning |
|---|---|---|---|

| The price rise costs more volume than E3 assumes | cfo, insurer, advocate | unknown / medium | Randomised online price test; monthly units against the 115,000 plan |
| "No subscription." stock sold after the change | advocate, homeowner | likely / high | Units from those packs keep full alerts for life; counsel review |

## 5. Open questions

- Cost of doing nothing in 2027: about 690,000 or up to 850,000 (not adjudicated; both rest on the
  unmeasured offline rate). The action does not depend on it.
- Whether a full subscription stays on the table for 2028: the cfo seat names a test that would
  bring it back; the homeowner and advocate seats treat it as a red line.
- Whether the platform bundle is priced per home (item 3), and how many buyers own a hub (V21).
- Every legal question (V22): features that differ by tier, free trials and cancellation,
  packaging claims on channel stock, alerts from a tenant's home, consent for alert data, a
  bundled insurance benefit. These go to qualified counsel in all four countries.

## 8. Limits of this council

- Simulated perspectives (separate subagents, parallel mode), not real buyers, landlords, insurers
  or competitors; the platform seat is an archetype. Missing seats: a retail partner, an investor
  or board seat for recurring revenue, a tenant; homeowner and advocate overlapped (tally E).
- Unverified: legal points, the insurer's claims figures, delivery rates, later packaging, what
  pilot non-payers lost (V22, V31, V33, V38). The verifier worked from source extracts.
- Real-world checks: buyer interviews (step 7), counsel (step 6), the full pilot and heartbeat
  data (steps 1-3).
